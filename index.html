Atmosphere

        

          

        

          

        

          

        
O₂, CO₂ and Rayleigh scale with surface pressure, so at 0 hPa they vanish — but H₂O does not follow (it is a fixed tropospheric column in the data), so zero its own slider too for a true vacuum.

      


      

        
Aerosol & scattering

        

          

        

          

        

          

        

          

        

          

        

          
          
        

        
With two-stream scattering on, the layer is coupled to the surface: photons taken out of the beam still reach the ground diffusely and still return, so a bright scattering aerosol barely dims the scene. Turn it off for extinction only and watch the A-band collapse — that is what the simple exp(−mτ) treatment gets wrong. Raising the layer shortens the path for atmospherically scattered photons, which is how aerosol biases retrieved XCO₂.

OCO-2 Spectra Explorer — model, equations and sources

      
      
    

    


      
What this is

      
An interactive line-by-line forward model of the three OCO-2 bands: the
      O₂ A-band (0.76 µm), the weak CO₂ band (1.61 µm) and the
      strong CO₂ band (2.06 µm). Sunlight crosses the atmosphere twice before
      reaching the satellite. In the A-band the absorber amount is known, so the band measures the
      light path — surface pressure, aerosol, cloud. The two CO₂ bands then reveal how
      much CO₂ that same path crossed.

      
It is an extended rewrite of the original
      OCO-2 Spectra Explorer created by Christian Frankenberg (Caltech/JPL),
      and reads the same pre-computed line-by-line data produced for it.

Forward model

      
τ_gas = psurf/P₀ · (XCO₂/420 · τ_co2 + τ_o2) + h2o · τ_h2o
τ_ray = 0.008569 µm⁻⁴ (1 + 0.0113 µm⁻² + 0.00013 µm⁻⁴) · psurf/P₀
τ_aer = AOD₅₅₀ · (λ/550)^(−å)
m      = 1/cos(SZA) + 1/cos(VZA)
a(λ)   = albScale · ECOSTRESS(λ) + albSlope · (ν − ν_centre),   ν = 10⁷/λ cm⁻¹

L   = (E₀ cosSZA / π) [ R · exp(−m·f·τ_gas) + T·a·T_diff · exp(−m·τ_gas) / (1 − a·s) ]
L_c = (E₀ cosSZA / π) [ R                  + T·a·T_diff                   / (1 − a·s) ]

      
P₀ = 1013.25 hPa. τ_h2o is deliberately not scaled by surface pressure — it is a
      fixed tropospheric column in the data, which says to scale only τ_co2 and τ_o2. The H₂O
      slider is a separate column scale factor, so at psurf = 0 you must also zero H₂O to reach a
      true vacuum.


      
Scattering

      
Aerosol and Rayleigh are mixed into one two-stream layer coupled to the Lambertian
      surface, not treated as pure extinction. The distinction is large: photons taken out of the
      direct beam are not lost, they still reach the ground diffusely and still come back up. At the
      bundled sounding’s AOD of 0.071, pure extinction dims the A-band 10.2 %; this
      treatment dims it 0.35 %.

      
    
        
  • Optical properties mix by the standard τ-weighted rule: τ adds, and ω₀ and g are
        τ-weighted between Rayleigh (ω₀ = 1, g = 0) and aerosol.
    • 
        
  • R, T come from delta-Eddington scaling
        (τ* = (1−ωg²)τ, ω* = ω(1−g²)/(1−ωg²), g* = g/(1+g))
        with the Meador & Weaver (1980) Eddington-closure collimated beam solution.
        s (spherical albedo) and T_diff come from the classical two-stream forms for
        diffuse illumination, used for the upward path off the surface.
    • 
        
  • f is the fraction of the well-mixed gas column above the layer, set by the
        layer pressure: f = p_layer/psurf. The atmospheric-scatter term crosses only that much gas.
        Rayleigh scattering is distributed ∝ pressure, so its mean scattering level sits at
        psurf/2, i.e. f = 0.5.
    • 
        
  • Accuracy is measured, not asserted: validate_scattering.py compares the
        system albedo against an independent Monte Carlo solution (Henyey–Greenstein phase
        function, Lambertian surface, collimated beam) over the full slider range. Worst absolute
        error 0.010; 0.6 % for the actual sounding case.
    • 
      

      
Turn two-stream scattering off to recover extinction-only and see the difference.


      
Instrument line shape

      
The radiance is formed at line-by-line resolution and then convolved with the real OCO-2
      ILS, evaluated as an edge-normalised correlation. Two things worth recording:

      
    
        
  • The kernel is placed using the tabulated delta_lambda_nm, i.e. index
        centre sits at Δλ = 0. The kernels are not centred on their array
        midpoint: in the A-band the true centre is 0.0090 nm away, 22 % of that band’s
        FWHM. Assuming the midpoint (as the original widget did) mis-registers the model and
        inflates the A-band residual against the real sounding from 3.88 to 6.60.
    • 
        
  • The convolution is evaluated only at the output positions rather than over the whole fine
        grid. That is bit-identical (verified: max abs diff 0.000e+00) and much cheaper. Sampling
        density follows the effective ILS width, so narrowing the ILS towards 0.1× resolves finer
        lines instead of aliasing them.
    • 
      


      
Spectral sorting (the “sorted” view)

      
Following Zeng et al. (2018). The channels are sorted into ascending order of
      clear-sky radiance — the same state with AOD = 0 — and that one ordering is
      then applied to every curve shown, so the scenes stay directly comparable. Strong, intermediate
      and weak lines therefore appear on the left, middle and right of the curve.

      
perm   = argsort( L_clear )          (ascending, AOD = 0, all else unchanged)
x-axis = sorted channel index
y-axis = radiance, reordered by perm

      
The physical basis, in the paper’s words: “atmospheric aerosols scatter photons back
      to space and therefore reduce the chance of the photons being absorbed by the oxygen, which is
      almost uniformly distributed in the atmosphere. Photons scattered by higher aerosol layers
      undergo shorter absorption paths, thereby reducing the O₂ absorption depths.” That is
      exactly the f factor above. Two pieces of information fall out:

      
    
        
  • Continuum (right) — O₂ absorption is weak, so radiance there tracks
        total AOD directly, given a fixed surface albedo.
    • 
        
  • Intermediate lines (middle) — these differ most between aerosol scenarios, so
        their level constrains aerosol layer height. Raising the layer lifts this section.
    • 
        
  • Saturated cores (left) — excluded in the paper’s retrieval, because
        saturation makes them measurement-noise dominated.
    • 
      

      
The shaded bands mark these three regions. The thresholds used here are clear-sky
      transmittance < 0.05, 0.05–0.80 and ≥ 0.80; they are a display convention for this
      tool, not the paper’s exact retrieval windows (those are in its supporting information).
      Note also that the paper works in the 1.27 µm O₂ band with a mountaintop FTS, and
      reports reflectance; here the same construction is applied to the OCO-2 bands in radiance.

      
One structural difference: the small wiggles the paper sees in its sorted radiance come from
      the O₂ absorption coefficients varying with pressure and temperature at different altitudes.
      This tool has a single fixed vertical τ profile, so it cannot reproduce that effect.


      
Approximations and limits

      
    
        
  • One homogeneous scattering layer, not a profile. It is a two-stream approximation,
        not a benchmark RT code.
    • 
        
  • The atmospheric path radiance is treated as isotropic — no phase-function
        angular dependence — and the diffuse surface path is charged the direct-beam gas slant
        path.
    • 
        
  • Spectral albedo is sampled at 5 nm: 5 points across the A-band, 10 across the
        strong-CO₂ band. Enough for a continuum tilt, not fine structure.
    • 
        
  • The albedo slope control is global, while L2 reports one per band.
        albedo_slope_l2_per_wn is not applied by “load L2 state”: its reference
        wavenumber is not recorded in the data, and measured against the sounding it does not help
        (the weak-CO₂ residual is already flat at 0.23 % trend and degrades to 1.44 % with it).
    • 
        
  • Noise is a display effect, σ = continuum/SNR, hashed deterministically on
        sample index so the curve does not shimmer while other sliders move.
    • 
        
  • Residuals against the real sounding will not go to zero. L2 Full Physics carries a
        multi-layer scattering atmosphere, retrieved aerosol types and profiles, temperature and water
        profiles, per-footprint dispersion and ILS, and zero-level offsets. Loading a few of its
        posterior values into a single-layer model with one Lambertian albedo per band cannot
        reproduce its radiances; the residual is dominated by that structural gap.
    • 
      


      
Verification

      
Add ?selftest=1 to the URL and this page will recompute its forward model at
      fixed points and compare against a NumPy reference generated by validate.py,
      reporting the worst relative deviation. It also renders every view × band × overlay
      combination to check no code path throws. validate_scattering.py is the Monte Carlo
      check of the scattering model. selftest_headless.js runs this page’s real script
      under gjs with a DOM shim, for CI without a browser.
